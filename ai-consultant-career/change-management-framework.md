## The method, attributed (OpenExO)

**SHAPE** — the human-architecture assessment. Five dimensions (Strategy, Human architecture, Adaptive architecture, Purpose, Ecosystem) scored 1–5; the Human dimension carries the change-management weight. Use it to diagnose where an org actually is before recommending adoption sequencing.

**The Binding Problem** — what retains high-judgment talent after the workflow changes. When AI collapses coordination cost it collapses exit cost in the same motion, so retention-by-friction is dead. Engineer retention-by-resonance on three levers (OpenExO's, attributed):
- *Consequence* — the person's judgment governs the new system; their leverage is the retention lever.
- *Legibility* — who-decided-what is visible by design.
- *Identity* — a purpose / role specific enough to exclude.
Compensation is hygiene: pay to parity, then stop competing there.

**People-Side budget envelope** — fund the transition from 10–15% of the savings the new workflow generates. The transition has a named leader and a real budget figure, or it is not serious. (This is why "I trained the team" with no budget is a tell.)

**Bridge Curriculum** — the funded training program that moves the coordination cohort into the new agent-augmented roles. Built for a porosity target (30%+) so the bridge is a real promotion path, not a consolation prize. This is where Alex's training / AI-literacy core meets the org-change method: the curriculum is the delivery vehicle for adoption.

**Parallel-run protocol** — old way and new way run side by side. The adoption signal is the human-override rate falling over time, not a training-completion count. Cut over only when the override rate stabilizes low.

**Absorption math / the Middle 60%** — honest modeling of how many people the new model actually holds. The cohort excellent at coordination is the one that gets absorbed or displaced; model it, don't assume it. If absorption hasn't been honestly modeled, the change case is incomplete.

**Self-Disruption Probe** — a structured exercise where the org names the workflow it would cannibalize first if it were a startup attacking itself. Feeds the adoption pipeline with the highest-leverage targets.

## Don't conflate Alex's pilot cohort with OpenExO's absorption cohort

Two different models answer two different questions — keep them separate:
- **Alex's 5 / 15 / 80** (Results 5%, Resilient 15%, Reluctant / Resistant 80%) — a *pilot-sequencing* model: start with the willing 20% to prove ROI fast, then scale. Alex's own; lives in `profile-data.md`. Use it for "where do I start the pilot."
- **OpenExO's Middle 60%** — an *absorption* model: the coordination-excellent cohort the new architecture absorbs or displaces in an org-wide rewrite. Use it for "what happens to the org at scale."

Mixing them is a variance bug: the willing-20% pilot cohort and the coordination-cohort absorption set are not the same people.
